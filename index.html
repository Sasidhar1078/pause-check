Three simple steps

What to do when something feels wrong

You are allowed to take your time. A caller who is genuine will understand if you hang up and check.

1

Pause

Do not reply, click a link, share a code, or send money while you feel pressured. End the call if you need to.

2

Check independently

Use a phone number on your bank card or official paperwork, or type the known website address yourself. Do not use the number or link in the suspicious message.

3

Ask someone you trust

Call a family member, friend, or caregiver using a number you already have. It is sensible to get a second opinion.

Know the warning signs

Scams often try to rush or frighten you

One warning sign is enough reason to stop and verify. Caller ID, a familiar logo, or a person’s voice is not proof by itself.

They demand secrecy or speed

“Act now,” “Do not tell anyone,” or “Your account will be blocked” are common pressure tactics.

They ask for private information

Never share your password, PIN, card security code, or one-time code with someone who contacts you.

They want an unusual payment

Be wary of requests for gift cards, cryptocurrency, cash collection, or a transfer to a “safe account.”

They send an unexpected link or attachment

A message may pretend to be from a delivery service, bank, government office, or someone you know. Check through a separate, trusted route.

Important: Scammers can fake a phone number, copy a company logo, and make a message sound personal. If in doubt, stop the conversation and contact the organisation or person yourself.

      A private self-check

      Does this call or message have warning signs?

      Tick anything that applies. This check stays on your device; it does not send your answers anywhere. It cannot prove whether a message is genuine.

      Did they do any of these things?

      If you already replied

      Act quickly, and remember: it is not your fault

      If you shared information, clicked a link, or sent money, taking action quickly can help. You do not need to feel embarrassed to ask for help.

      If you shared bank details or sent money

      Call your bank using the number on your card or statement. Ask them to secure your account and explain the payment. Contact your local police or official fraud-reporting service.

      If you shared a password or code

      Use the official app or website to change the password. If you reuse it elsewhere, change it there too. Contact the relevant organisation using a trusted number.

      If you clicked a link or installed an app

      Do not enter more information. If you installed an app at their request, ask a trusted person or your device provider to help remove it and check your device.

      Get support

      It is okay to ask for help

      Contact your bank using a number printed on your card or statement. You can also speak with a trusted family member, friend, caregiver, or local community support service.

      If you are in India

      If money has been lost to an online financial fraud, contact your bank promptly and report it to the National Cyber Crime Helpline by calling 1930. You can also use the official portal at cybercrime.gov.in. Use official channels; do not pay anyone who promises to recover your money.
